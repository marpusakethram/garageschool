How a VC thinks, in five ideas

Adapted for founders from a talk at Stanford by a seed investor who uses decision analysis to pick companies.

  1. Outliers pay for everything

    A tiny share of deals produces nearly all venture profit. Investors look first for a believable path to a very large outcome.

  2. Scenarios, not certainty

    Good investors weigh possible outcomes by their odds. Help them with honest ranges, not promises.

  3. A few things matter

    Five or six assumptions drive the decision. Know yours, and bring evidence on exactly those.

  4. Stages of risk

    Early success, crossing to mainstream customers, then the mass market. Show proof for the next stage.

  5. A no is often about the fund

    Stage, portfolio math, and follow-on rules decide many passes. Learn the fund before you pitch it.

    Practice the interview

    A few of the questions founders hear most. Answer each one out loud in under 30 seconds before opening the notes.

    What does your company do?

    What they are testing: whether you can explain it simply. A strong answer: one sentence naming the customer and the product in plain words.

    How do you know people want this?

    What they are testing: evidence of demand versus enthusiasm. A strong answer: behavior, not opinions: paying customers, usage, retention, people who found you on their own.

    What is your retention?

    What they are testing: whether customers stay, the core signal of product-market fit. A strong answer: churn or cohort retention with numbers, and why customers leave when they do.

    How big can this get?

    What they are testing: whether a very large outcome is possible. A strong answer: a bottom-up estimate with your reasoning, plus the path to adjacent markets.

    What is the biggest risk to this company?

    What they are testing: self-awareness and honesty. A strong answer: the real risk, named plainly, and the evidence you are gathering to reduce it.

    How much are you raising, and what will you do with it?

    What they are testing: planning and ambition. A strong answer: the amount, the runway it buys, and the milestone it reaches.
